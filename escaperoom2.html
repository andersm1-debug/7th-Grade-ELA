Lock 1: RL.7.5 (Poetry Structure)

The Musical Brook

Scenario: Analyze how rhyme scheme contributes to structure. Read the excerpt from The Brook by Alfred, Lord Tennyson.

I chatter over stony ways,
In little sharps and trebles,
I bubble into eddying bays,
I babble on the pebbles.

The Mission Question: How does the consistent, bouncing rhyme scheme (ways/bays, trebles/pebbles) contribute to the overall structure of the poem?

  • A: By conveying the destructive and violent force of a flood.
  • B: By creating a quick, musical rhythm that mimics the physical flowing of water over rocks.
  • C: By showing that nature is disorganized and refuses to follow predictable patterns.
  • D: By confusing the reader to represent how easily one can get lost in the woods.

Select the correct letter. Then, find the plural noun at the end of the stanza that refers to small stones. Type that exact word below.

Lock 3: RL.7.5 (Structural Contrast)

The Eternal River

Scenario: Evaluate contrasting structures in poetry. Read the final lines of The Brook.

I chatter, chatter, as I flow
To join the brimming river,
For men may come and men may go,
But I go on for ever.

The Mission Question: How does the structural contrast in the final two lines contribute to the poem's meaning?

  • A: It shows that humans are faster and more active than the slow-moving river.
  • B: It proves that nature relies on humans to keep rivers clean.
  • C: It highlights the brook's sadness over being left alone by travelers.
  • D: It emphasizes the brief, temporary nature of human life compared to the eternal lifespan of nature.

🔑 Lock Code Task:

Select the correct letter. Then, find the adjective used to describe the full "river" the brook joins. Type that word.

Lock 4: RL.7.1 (Inferencing)

The Hidden Anger

Scenario: Analyze the inception of a conflict. Read the first stanza of A Poison Tree by William Blake.

I was angry with my friend;
I told my wrath, my wrath did end.
I was angry with my foe:
I told it not, my wrath did grow.

The Mission Question: What can be inferred from the first stanza of the poem?

  • A: The speaker easily forgives everyone they meet.
  • B: Open communication resolves anger, while hiding it causes it to multiply.
  • C: The speaker's enemy forced them to stay silent.
  • D: It is impossible to ever truly make peace with a friend.

🔑 Lock Code Task:

Select the correct letter. Then, find the noun repeated three times in this stanza that means "extreme anger."

Lock 5: RL.7.5 (Stanza Progression)

The Toxic Garden

Scenario: Track the progression of a metaphor. Read the second stanza of A Poison Tree.

And I waterd it in fears,
Night & morning with my tears:
And I sunned it with smiles,
And with soft deceitful wiles.

The Mission Question: Why does the poet start nearly every line in this stanza with the word "And"?

  • A: It creates a structural "snowball" effect, emphasizing the relentless, building growth of the speaker's anger.
  • B: It forces the reader to pause and reflect on the speaker's sadness.
  • C: It breaks the rhyme scheme to show the speaker is losing control.
  • D: It shows that the speaker is changing their mind about seeking revenge.

🔑 Lock Code Task:

Select the correct letter. Then, find the plural noun in line 2 that the speaker uses to "water" the tree morning and night.
