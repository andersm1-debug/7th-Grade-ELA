Edition 2: AI as a Writing Partner

Hi Team,

We know the elephant in the room: if AI can write a perfect essay in three seconds, what happens to our writing assignments?

It is time to shift the narrative. AI is not just a shortcut for students; it can be a collaborative tool for both the drafting process and our own grading workflows.

The “Work Smarter” Spotlight

Here are four ways to use AI as a writing partner.

  • The “Critique the Bot” Method: Project a generic AI paragraph and have students humanize it with sensory details, stronger vocabulary, and varied sentence structure.
  • The “Targeted Feedback Buddy”: Teach students to ask AI for focused revision feedback, such as checking transitions or evidence, before submitting a draft.
  • The “Exemplar Creator”: Use AI to generate leveled examples of writing so students can see what Level 1, 2, 3, and 4 responses look like.
  • The “Rubric Evaluator”: Paste in your rubric and a student draft to get preliminary scoring notes and targeted feedback aligned to your criteria.

The AI Coach’s Toolbox

Copy and paste these prompts when you want to support writing instruction without starting from scratch.

Prompt 1: The “Bland Baseline” Generator

Write a structurally perfect but incredibly boring and generic 1-paragraph response to the following prompt: [Insert Writing Prompt Here]. Do not use sensory details, varied sentence structure, or strong vocabulary. Make it sound like a robot wrote it so my students can practice revising and humanizing it.

Prompt 2: The “Feedback Buddy”

You are an expert writing tutor. Review the following draft for a [Insert Essay Type]. Do NOT rewrite the essay for me. Instead, tell me if my thesis statement is clear, and point out two places where I need more evidence to support my claim. Here is my draft: [Paste Draft]

Prompt 3: The “Exemplar Creator”

I am grading a [Insert Grade Level & Subject] assignment. The prompt is: [Insert Prompt]. Please generate three short example essays for my students to read: one that represents a low-performing Level 2 response, one that is an average Level 3 response, and one that is an exceptional Level 4 response. Highlight the key differences between the three.

Prompt 4: The “Rubric Evaluator”

You are an expert middle school teacher. I am going to provide you with my grading rubric and a student's essay. Please evaluate the essay based strictly on the rubric categories provided. For your response: 1. Provide a score for each category. 2. Quote specific evidence from the student's text to justify the score. 3. Offer two actionable steps for improvement. Rubric: [Paste Your Rubric Here] Student Essay: [Paste Student Draft Here]
