RI.7.5: Text Structure

🗝️ Locker #2: "Walking Through the World of Math"

Context Excerpt: "...The golden ratio is found in the arrangement of seeds in a sunflower. But wait, there’s more. This ratio is also related to another famous concept: the Fibonacci sequence. Ready for even more? This sequence doesn't just show up in nature; it's also found in music and architecture..."

Question: How does the author’s use of the phrases “But wait, there’s more” and “Ready for even more?” affect the structure of the selection?

  • A. They emphasize that the author thinks math can be exhausting.
  • B. They signal that the author is about to change the topic.
  • C. They provide a transition to the next idea in the selection. (Correct)
  • D. They highlight the author’s opinion that math is a difficult subject.
🔓 Unlock the Test-Maker's Rationale

The Trap: Options A and D are "Tone Traps." They try to trick you into picking a negative feeling. However, the author is actually excited about math! Option B is wrong because the author stays on the same topic—math in the real world.

The Win: These phrases are Road Signs. They prepare your brain for a new example that builds on the one before it. In Standard RI.7.5, we call these structural transitions.
