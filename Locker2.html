🎯 EOG Strategy: The Rationale Locker

Analyze how text-makers build a passage to trick your brain.

Target Standard: RI.7.5 (Text Structure)

🗝️ Locker #2: "Walking Through the World of Math"

Context Excerpt: "...The golden ratio is found in the arrangement of seeds in a sunflower. But wait, there’s more. This ratio is also related to another famous concept: the Fibonacci sequence. Ready for even more? This sequence doesn't just show up in nature; it's also found in music and architecture..."

The Question: How does the author’s use of the phrases “But wait, there’s more” and “Ready for even more?” affect the structure of the selection?

  • A. They emphasize that the author thinks math can be exhausting.
  • B. They signal that the author is about to change the topic.
  • C. They provide a transition to the next idea in the selection. (Correct)
  • D. They highlight the author’s opinion that math is a difficult subject.
🔓 Unlock the Test-Maker's Rationale

The Trap: Options A and D are "Tone Traps." They try to trick you into picking a negative feeling (exhausting/difficult) because many people find math hard. However, the author’s tone is actually enthusiastic!

The Distractor: Option B is wrong because the author isn't changing the topic; they are staying on the same topic and simply adding more examples.

The Win: These phrases are "Road Signs." Just like a sign on the highway tells you what's coming, these phrases prepare your brain for a new piece of info that builds on the last one. In Standard RI.7.5, we call these structural transitions.
