🎯 EOG Strategy: The Rationale Locker

Analyzing Drama Structure & Stage Directions (RL.7.5).

Target Standard: RL.7.5 (Drama Structure)

🗝️ Locker #4: "The Monsters Are Due on Maple Street"

Exerpt from Scene 1:

(SCENE: MAPLE STREET, U.S.A., LATE SUMMER. It is a tree-lined, quiet, residential street. We see a man washing his car. Another man is putting up a screen door. We hear the sound of a lawnmower in the distance. It is a scene of absolute normalcy.)

STEVE: (Looking up) What was that?

(Suddenly, there is a loud, screeching roar and a flash of light across the sky. The man stops washing his car. The man on the porch stops his work. They all look up at the sky. As the light fades, the camera zooms in on their faces, showing a transition from curiosity to growing unease.)

MRS. BRAND: (Quietly) Steve? What happened? The power... it just went off.

The Question: In the opening scene, how do the stage directions and sound cues most contribute to the meaning of the play?

  • A. They explain the scientific reason why the power goes out later in the story.
  • B. They establish a sense of 'normalcy' that is suddenly shattered, creating immediate suspense for the audience. (Correct)
  • C. They provide the actors with a checklist of movements to ensure they stay in character.
  • D. They offer a summary of the plot so the audience knows what to expect at the end.
🔓 Unlock the Test-Maker's Rationale

The Trap: Option C is the most common mistake. Yes, stage directions tell actors what to do, but the EOG wants to know why that matters to the story. Choosing the most literal answer is often a trap!

The Distractor: Option A is wrong because stage directions in this play are meant to be mysterious, not scientific. They raise questions rather than giving answers.

The Win: The "Meaning" (Standard RL.7.5) comes from the Contrast. By describing a perfect, boring summer day and then suddenly interrupting it with a "screeching roar," Rod Serling makes the audience feel exactly what the characters feel: that something very wrong is about to happen. This structural choice builds the suspense that carries the whole play.
