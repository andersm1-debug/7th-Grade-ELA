Group 3: The Desert Catcher

Strategic Scaffold • Problem & Solution

[¶1] The Namib Desert is one of the driest places on Earth. It almost never rains, and the sun beats down relentlessly. Most animals would quickly die of thirst in this harsh landscape. However, the tiny Namib Desert beetle has a special trick to survive without drinking from puddles or streams.

[¶2] Every morning, a thick fog rolls in from the nearby ocean. The beetle climbs to the top of a sand dune and tilts its body forward into the wind. Its bumpy back is designed to catch the fog. The tiny bumps attract water droplets from the wet air, while the smooth sides of the bumps repel the water.

[¶3] As the fog passes over the beetle, heavy water droplets form on its shell. Gravity pulls the drops down the beetle’s tilted back, sliding them straight into its waiting mouth. Thanks to this clever adaptation, the beetle drinks fresh water every single day in the middle of a bone-dry desert!
