🎯 EOG Strategy: The Rationale Locker

Unlocking the structure of Poetry (RL.7.5).

Target Standard: RL.7.5 (Poetry Structure)

🗝️ Locker #3: "Annabel Lee" by Edgar Allan Poe

Context Excerpt: It was many and many a year ago, In a kingdom by the sea, That a maiden there lived whom you may know By the name of Annabel Lee; [...] But we loved with a love that was more than love— I and my Annabel Lee— With a love that the wingèd seraphs of Heaven Coveted her and me. And this was the reason that, long ago, In this kingdom by the sea...

The Question: How does the repetition of the phrase “In this kingdom by the sea” throughout the poem contribute to its structure and meaning?

  • A. It emphasizes that the narrator is planning to move away from the coast.
  • B. It shows that the narrator’s main conflict is with the ocean.
  • C. It creates a rhythmic refrain that reinforces the fairy-tale-like setting and the haunting nature of the narrator's memory. (Correct)
  • D. It suggests that the narrator is trying to remember where he met Annabel Lee.
🔓 Unlock the Test-Maker's Rationale

The Trap: Options A and D treat the poem like a literal story about travel or memory loss. The EOG wants to see if you can think beyond the plot and look at the art of the writing.

The Distractor: Option B is tricky because it mentions "conflict," which is a story element, but the repetition isn't there to show he's mad at the water.

The Win: Repetition in a poem creates a Refrain. Just like a chorus in a song, it anchors the reader. By repeating the "Kingdom," Poe makes the story feel like a myth or a fable. This structural choice (Standard RL.7.5) makes the narrator's love feel legendary and timeless.
