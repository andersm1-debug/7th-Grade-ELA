Group 3: The Sunken Forest

Strategic Scaffold • Cause/Effect Chain

[¶1] Deep beneath the waters of Lake Washington sits an ancient secret. Over 1,000 years ago, a massive earthquake shook the Seattle coast. The violent shaking triggered a huge landslide, sending an entire hillside of Douglas fir trees sliding into the deep lake.[cite: 8]

[¶2] In most lakes, submerged wood rots quickly because bacteria and oxygen break it down. But this lake water is freezing, dark, and lacks oxygen near the bottom. Because of these conditions, the trees never decayed. Today, dozens of ancient fir trees still stand upright under 200 feet of water like stone pillars.[cite: 8]

[¶3] Marine scientists now use underwater robotic sonar to map the grove. By studying samples of the preserved tree rings, researchers can pinpoint the exact season the historic earthquake struck, helping engineers design safer modern buildings today.[cite: 8]
